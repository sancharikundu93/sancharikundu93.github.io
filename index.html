Biography

Sanchari Kundu

Ph.D. Candidate · Virginia Tech

Reading how plants respond to stress, one cell type at a time.

Google Scholar GitHub Email CV
As a plant single-cell biologist, I develop single-cell and spatial transcriptomic approaches to understand how individual cell types respond to environmental stress. Working at the interface of wet lab and dry lab, I combine snRNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, imaging and cross-species annotation to link gene function to stress resilience. My long-term goal is to apply omics approaches to food crops and medicinal plants to support food security and human health.

Sanchari Kundu is a Ph.D. candidate in Crop and Soil Environmental Sciences in the School of Plant and Environmental Sciences at Virginia Tech. Her research dissects cell-type-specific transcriptional responses to abiotic stress. Using single-nucleus RNA-seq, she studies how individual cell types in pennycress (Thlaspi arvense) roots remodel under salt stress, including alternative splicing and polyadenylation. She also develops computational methods for non-model plants: refining 3′ UTR annotation to improve single-cell quantification across ten species, expanding the Orthogroup Marker Gene (OMG) resource for cross-species cell-type annotation, and building a pathway-enrichment-based approach to annotate Stereo-seq spatial transcriptomics data in pepper fruit.

Sanchari's path into plant science began in India. She received her B.Sc. in Microbiology from West Bengal University of Technology, graduating with a silver medal, and her M.Sc. in Agricultural Biotechnology from Ramakrishna Mission Vivekananda University, where she carried out an in-silico study of retrotransposons in bamboo. She then moved to the United States and earned an M.S. in Integrated and Organismal Biology at the University of New Hampshire, where she studied how the polyamines putrescine and spermidine help hybrid poplar tolerate salt and cadmium stress.

She joined Virginia Tech in 2023, moving from whole-plant physiology to single-cell and spatial genomics. She has presented her work at the Gordon Research Conference on Single Cell Approaches in Plant Biology, the Plant and Animal Genome Conference and the Salk Institute's Discover Symposium, and has helped lead the Summer Single Cell Workshop at Virginia Tech. She is currently seeking a postdoctoral position in computational plant genomics.
