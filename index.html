Plant Single-Cell Biologist & Computational Genomics

Sanchari Kundu

I study how individual plant cells respond to environmental stress. Working at the interface of wet lab and dry lab, I combine single-nucleus and spatial transcriptomics, imaging and cross-species annotation to link gene function to stress resilience, with the goal of building more resilient food crops.

About

Sanchari Kundu

Ph.D. Candidate · Virginia Tech

Raised in India, I earned my B.Sc. in Microbiology from West Bengal University of Technology, graduating with a silver medal, and my M.Sc. in Agricultural Biotechnology from Ramakrishna Mission Vivekananda University, where I carried out an in-silico study of retrotransposons in bamboo. I then moved to the United States for an M.S. in Integrated and Organismal Biology at the University of New Hampshire, where I studied how the polyamines putrescine and spermidine help hybrid poplar tolerate salt and cadmium stress.

In 2023 I joined the School of Plant and Environmental Sciences at Virginia Tech as a Ph.D. student in Crop and Soil Environmental Sciences, moving from whole-plant physiology to single-cell and spatial genomics. Using single-nucleus RNA-seq, I study how individual cell types in pennycress (Thlaspi arvense) roots remodel under salt stress, including alternative splicing and polyadenylation. I also develop computational methods for non-model plants: refining 3′ UTR annotation to improve single-cell quantification across ten species, expanding the Orthogroup Marker Gene (OMG) resource for cross-species cell-type annotation, and building a pathway-enrichment-based approach to annotate Stereo-seq spatial transcriptomics data in pepper fruit.

Research Experience

2023 – present
Ph.D. Graduate Researcher, School of Plant and Environmental Sciences, Virginia Tech (Blacksburg, VA, USA).
  • Cell-type-specific transcriptional responses to salt stress in pennycress roots: snRNA-seq under salinity resolving cell-type responses and alternative splicing / polyadenylation isoform diversity; bulk RNA-seq temporal deconvolution; confocal and brightfield root imaging; growth-chamber experiments.
  • Refining 3′ UTR annotation to improve scRNA-seq quantification in ten non-model plants.
  • Cross-species spatial annotation for pepper (Stereo-seq) using pathway-specific enrichment to identify cell types and tissue domains.
  • OMG 2.0: scaling the Orthogroup Marker Gene annotation method and building a web resource with organ- and condition-specific marker features.
  • Spatial maps from single-cell data: confocal imaging of Arabidopsis fluorescent marker lines to build a 2D root model and map scRNA-seq data.
Research keywords: single-nucleus RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, salt stress, cross-species annotation, pennycress (Thlaspi arvense), pepper (Capsicum), Arabidopsis thaliana.
2018 – 2022
Graduate Researcher, Department of Biological Sciences, University of New Hampshire (Durham, NH, USA).
  • Role of putrescine and spermidine in salt and cadmium tolerance in hybrid poplar (clone NM6): gas exchange (LI-COR 6400) and HPLC quantification of sugars, polyamines, phytochelatins and amino acids.
Research keywords: polyamines, abiotic stress, plant physiology, hybrid poplar (Populus).
