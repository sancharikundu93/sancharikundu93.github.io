About

Sanchari Kundu

Ph.D. Candidate · Virginia Tech

Raised in India, I earned my B.Sc. in Microbiology from West Bengal University of Technology, graduating with a silver medal, and my M.Sc. in Agricultural Biotechnology from Ramakrishna Mission Vivekananda University, where I carried out an in-silico study of retrotransposons in bamboo. I then moved to the United States for an M.S. in Integrated and Organismal Biology at the University of New Hampshire, where I studied how the polyamines putrescine and spermidine help hybrid poplar tolerate salt and cadmium stress.

In 2023 I joined the School of Plant and Environmental Sciences at Virginia Tech as a Ph.D. student in Crop and Soil Environmental Sciences, moving from whole-plant physiology to single-cell and spatial genomics. Using single-nucleus RNA-seq, I study how individual cell types in pennycress (Thlaspi arvense) roots remodel under salt stress, including alternative splicing and polyadenylation. I also develop computational methods for non-model plants: refining 3′ UTR annotation to improve single-cell quantification across ten species, expanding the Orthogroup Marker Gene (OMG) resource for cross-species cell-type annotation, and building a pathway-enrichment-based approach to annotate Stereo-seq spatial transcriptomics data in pepper fruit.
